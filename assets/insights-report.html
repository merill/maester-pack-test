Demo pack insights

The Maester report already says which checks passed and failed. A pack's own report is the place for what a list of results cannot show: how much there is, how it is spread, how it changes from run to run, and where the outliers are. Each card below is one idea, with a note on why it is drawn that way.

A report is optional. A Maester pack does not have to include one: its test results already appear in the Maester report. This page only shows what you can add if your pack collects data worth charting.

At a glance
Identity

Strongest sign-in method per member

Idea: a doughnut for part of a whole. A test can pass while 65 people still have no method at all; the mix shows how far the tenant is from the goal. Keep it to six slices or fewer, and use a bar chart when the values are close.

MFA over the last 12 weeks

Idea: a line for change over time. A result is a snapshot; a trend says whether things are getting better. Keep the history in the pack's own store, or read it from the tenant (sign-in logs, audit logs, reports), and put both series on one axis only when they share a unit.

Accounts by last sign-in

Idea: a histogram with the part that matters picked out. A test checks a threshold (90 days); the shape shows how many are just under it and will cross it next month. One colour for the buckets past the threshold, grey for the rest.

Admin sign-ins by day and hour (UTC)

Idea: a heatmap for patterns in time. No library: a CSS grid with one shade of blue, darker for more. Odd-hour activity stands out at a glance, which no single pass/fail result would show.

Devices, apps and sharing

Device compliance by operating system

Idea: 100% stacked bars to compare shares across groups of different sizes. The counts are in the tooltip and the table. Status colours (green, red) are used only because they mean good and bad here, and the legend names them.

Apps by permissions and users

Idea: a bubble chart to find outliers in two dimensions. Many permissions and few users, at the bottom right, is the corner to look at; the size of a bubble is its sign-ins. Keep it to two or three colours.

Score by area, now and three months ago

Idea: a radar for a profile across areas. It shows balance: which area lags the others. Use it for five to eight areas on the same 0-100 scale; for exact comparisons, a grouped bar chart reads better.

Groups with the most guests

    Idea: a ranked list with inline bars. No library: a list and a CSS bar. The bar is the share of guests in the group, so a small group that is mostly guests ranks next to a big one.

    This run

    Time to collect each kind of data

    Idea: report on the run itself. These are the real times of this run. Slow sources and failures are what a person needs when a run takes too long or misses data.

    Charts drawn with Chart.js 4.5.1 (MIT licence), inline in this file. The heatmap and the ranked list are plain HTML and CSS. Every number except the run times is made up.
